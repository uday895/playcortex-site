Siddh Sehrawat

Seven Years of Archery, and One Wrong Digit

"I felt really alive, bro."

Siddh Sehrawat took up archery in sixth grade in 2015, alongside his younger brother. Their father wanted both boys to take one sport seriously, and they had already tried basketball, badminton and swimming. An uncle visiting Chandigarh suggested archery because it was an individual sport, and their mother signed them up at Lake Club.

Six Months of Rubber Bands

For the first six months they weren't allowed to shoot. They stood in one spot for hours pulling a rubber band to build strength and form. "It's extremely boring." The brothers quit and came back several times. In ninth grade they finally committed, training three to four hours every evening and doing their running and push-ups together.

In eighth or ninth grade he won two golds and a silver at the CBSE North Zone meet. After that his mother moved them from the government centre to a private coach. That meant six more months of just pulling the bow before he could shoot again. His scores jumped from around 280 out of 360 to 330-345, and his national ranking climbed into the top 10-12.

The Asia Cup Trials

Covid wiped out his Under-14 years. Back in competition, in tenth grade he won gold in both the Under-14 and Under-17 categories at the state championship in Moga, and bronze in Under-19.

After his board exams he had three months to prepare for the Asia Cup trials. His coach told him his chances were slim. Siddh practically lived at the ground, sleeping at a friend's house while his mother brought him food. At the trials in Sonipat he got through the cuts to the top 32, then the top 24, then the top 16. "I felt really alive, bro."

A Five Instead of a Six

He left the final scoring sure that he had made the top nine, which would have meant a Khelo India place, a scholarship and a shot at the Asia Cup. When the list came out, he was sixteenth. Archers wrote down their own scores, and he had written a 5 where he should have written a 6, which cost him 100 points.

His game was never quite the same after that. Moving up to 70 metres was hard, and a fourth place at the National School Games in twelfth grade came with no medal.

A New Discipline

After seven years he decided to treat archery as a finished chapter, though he has kept his bow. He took up pistol shooting, and his coach says his aim and focus from archery have helped him progress faster than other beginners. He has already been given a personal pistol.

He is now studying B.Com LLB at UILS, aiming for the judiciary or the IPS, with sport still part of the plan. Dedicated, consistent, serious and hard-working are the words he uses for himself.
