Sourabh

The goalkeeper they called Sandy

"I just want to stay in football. I can open my own academy. I can be a coach."

When the penalty shootout began, half the stadium was chanting his name. Sandy, Sandy, Sandy. For Sourabh, a 22-year-old goalkeeper from a middle-class Delhi family with roots in Bihar, it was the loudest proof yet that a boy who started in a local park, against his father's wishes, could become the player a whole college was counting on.

Street football and a no from home

Sourabh's football began as street football in a nearby park, the game every child plays after school and tuition. His first school team trial came in 2016 and 2017, at a government school where the team existed mostly on paper. Even so, they won a sub-junior tournament, and something in him lit up.

When he told his parents he wanted to be a football player, his father said no. Football was a game for fun, not a career, and he wanted his son to take science. His father did not speak to him for a few days. His mother, though, backed him. After his Class 10 board exams, Sourabh went to the government academy at the Jawaharlal Nehru Stadium on his own, filled in the forms and got himself admitted.

Fearless in a crowded academy

The academy was packed, with barely room to take a step, and training ran from late morning into the night. What made Sourabh stand out, his coaches noticed, was that he had no fear. He trained with senior players and service team players, travelled with the team even when he sat on the bench, and learned constantly.

Looking for better support for his sport, he moved schools and spent months practising alone on an empty ground for School Games Federation of India trials. After a long, difficult selection process he was picked in the under-17 age group. It was his turning point, and it was also the moment his father told him he was proud. When the pandemic hit, a contact at his new school helped him secure a full scholarship, allowing him to keep studying and training. In 2022, in Class 12, his school team won the Delhi state championship without conceding a single goal.

The college detour

College admission brought heartbreak at first. While friends got into their dream colleges, Sourabh landed at Shaheed Bhagat Singh College. He was upset, but that year gave him friends and valuable training under a top goalkeeper. Then came an unexpected chance to move to Hindu College, his dream institution. On the very last day of the migration portal, with offices closed, a friend told him to go straight to the principal, who recognised what the opportunity meant and signed off. Sourabh remembers running back to the metro station that afternoon.

At Hindu College, studying Political Science Honours, he faced academic pressure and even whispers that he must have had connections to get in. He remains deeply grateful to the staff who mentored him through that move.

The shootout

In 2023 the Hindu College team, with only four sports quota players and the rest academic students, went on a memorable run. They beat SRCC, fought through a tough match against IGIPESS and reached the decisive stages. In a high-pressure shootout against Venkateswara College, in front of a stadium split between the two sides, Sourabh talked to the takers, tried to plant doubt, and saved penalties. Hindu College defended its championship and qualified for the All-India level for the first time in 18 years. He calls 2023 a magical year.

Injury, recovery and a life in football

In January 2026, Sourabh suffered an ACL injury. He is now walking and jogging lightly, not yet running, and determined to return as soon as possible. He never used supplements before the injury and still scored 100 percent in fitness tests, but he has started using them in recovery, alongside guidance from sports injury specialists. He changed his diet years ago on a coach's advice, adding chicken and eggs. He knows how many young players train without proper meals, and believes parents and academies must step in.

The injury has been hard on his mind too, and he speaks openly about wanting better mental support for athletes. His plans are clear: postgraduate study at a university where he can keep playing, and then a life that stays in football, whether coaching, sports management or opening his own academy. He cannot imagine a life without the game. A friend once told him he was nothing to everything, and he has carried that line with him ever since.
