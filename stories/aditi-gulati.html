Aditi Gulati

Humming her way through the hard points

"Everything that I am right now is just because of my hard work and my family's hard work."

Aditi Gulati describes herself, with disarming honesty, as nobody's idea of a gifted prodigy. What she does have is a work ethic built over two decades, a family that has travelled every step with her, and a stubbornness that has carried her from a residential complex in Gurgaon to the US college tennis system. A tennis player based in Dubai and now studying in New York, she is trying to build a professional tennis career without giving up her education.

A racket she did not want

Her tennis story starts with a confession. At five years old, living in a small complex in Gurgaon, her father handed her a tennis racket, a golf club and a football, and she and her brothers played all three. Aditi, by her own account, hated tennis. Her father told the coach who worked in the complex that he would not be paid if she skipped class. It happened once, and after that the coach would come and ring the doorbell to bring her to practice. By around seven, she had settled on tennis while her siblings went towards golf.

When the family moved to Dubai, the sport became serious. Her first coach there was Ron Manuja, who had played Davis Cup for India. She competed in the under-10, under-12, under-14 and under-18 categories at the same time, with her mother sitting through entire weekends of matches. She won the under-10 and under-12 categories of a junior masters series whose prize was a fully paid trip to Paris to watch Roland Garros.

Going international

During the pandemic she trained under a coach whose methods were so intense she joked it felt like military training, and she eventually realised she had outgrown being yelled at across the net. At 14, in 2021, she played her first international event in Ghana, then travelled to Togo, where she earned her first ITF points. Later, under a Serbian coach named Milan at the Top Spin academy, she won ITF titles and made deep runs in tournaments. More recently she has trained with Vera Zvonareva, a former world number two, and she gives special credit to John Guntley, the coach who helped her truly love the game.

The humbling road to college

Her parents, a school principal and a lawyer, were clear from the start that a degree was not optional. Her father, a former professional squash player who handled the sport side and travelled with her, was equally clear that she should not quit after all that work. Her mother managed school deadlines and drew up five-year plans, always with a realistic Plan B.

That combination led to US college recruitment, a process Aditi calls humbling. She attended ID camps at Lehigh and Harvard, received a Division 1 offer from George Washington University and a scholarship offer from UMass Amherst, and learned how hard it is for an international player to stand out. After a series of twists in admissions, she enrolled at Pomona College, played there for a semester, and then decided to transfer to Columbia University to give herself a Division 1 environment and a better shot at turning professional.

Fighting her own body

Injuries have been the hardest opponent. Over the years she has dealt with shoulder tendinitis, knee problems, torn ligaments, ankle injuries and, most recently, stress fractures in both legs with torn fascia. Doctors in the US told her surgery was certain. A doctor in Dubai took a different view, and with injections, therapy and around 16 physio sessions, she was back on court for the first time in two months. Her rule through it all is simple: the worst thing you can do is be negative, even if it is much easier said than done.

Bollywood between points

Tennis, Aditi says, is a lonely sport, and her mind can run away from her mid-match. Her fix is wonderfully her own. She reminds herself to watch the ball at the contact point, and between points she walks back to the fence and hums a song, usually an upbeat Bollywood number, so she stays loose rather than sinking into pressure.

Off court she is an entrepreneur at heart, building an app for college use and exploring a real-time tennis coaching app. Her short-term goals are to improve her rating and ranking and to start playing pro events on the ITF circuit. If tennis does not take her all the way, she sees herself in management consulting and, eventually, a startup that sticks. Either way, her philosophy is the same: you need to have fun in anything you do.
