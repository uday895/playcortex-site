Sandeepti Singh Rao

A Love-and-Hate Relationship With the Game

"Tennis is something I truly cannot live without."

Sandeepti Singh Rao was a hyperactive child who found it hard to sit still. Her mother enrolled her at a nearby tennis academy to give that energy somewhere to go. Nobody was trying to make her a professional. Tennis was just fun.

The First Win

The first change came when she won a district-level tournament. That win showed her parents how competitive she was, how well she handled pressure and how much she cared about the game. They found her a better academy, and tennis gradually became the centre of her life.

Training got harder, expectations rose, and she reached one of the best phases of her career. Then, at her peak, she suffered a major knee injury that needed surgery.

The Questions on Repeat

The injury hit her mind as hard as her body. The same questions kept running through her head. Would she compete again? Would she ever play at the same level? Would her body feel the same after surgery? What if it happened again? "These thoughts were exhausting and emotionally draining, and they often felt heavier than the physical pain itself."

She cried a lot, and at her lowest she thought about quitting. Her parents kept believing in her when she had stopped believing in herself. "My comeback, in many ways, belongs to them as much as it belongs to me."

A Stick and a Knee Brace

The restrictions lasted a long time. She couldn't ice skate or go on trampolines, and she went to college on a walking stick and a knee brace. Having to be careful with her leg all the time made her anxious.

Rehab was long and painful, and some days she hated every minute. She kept showing up even when she had no motivation, because she believed giving up would hurt more than trying.

More Than Wins and Losses

Today she has a love-and-hate relationship with tennis, but she can't imagine her life without it. It made her strong, taught her resilience and gave her an identity. She also believes many people in India still don't understand the mental side of being an athlete: injuries, setbacks and pressure affect confidence and emotional wellbeing as much as the body.

For her, tennis is about who she has become as much as about winning or losing.
