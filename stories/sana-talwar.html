Sana Talwar

Fast, the Very Thing She Once Wasn't

"I don't think I'll ever actually quit."

Sana Talwar's start in basketball had nothing dramatic about it. In second grade, her friends encouraged her to stay back for practice, and she went along. She'd always been athletic, and her father had always pushed her toward trying new sports, so it seemed like a natural fit. What followed instead was a long stretch of disappointment. She was short, not especially fast, and asthmatic, and from second grade through fifth or sixth grade, she couldn't make a single team, not even at school. "Mostly feeling disheartened," is how she remembers those years. "I loved the sport but kept missing out."

What Lockdown Changed

The shift came, quite unexpectedly, during Covid. Before lockdown, she'd asked her coach directly why she wasn't making the team, and he told her plainly that she wasn't fast enough yet and needed more skill work. During the shutdown, she used the time well. Despite being severely asthmatic, and then facing a COVID infection that further reduced her lung capacity, she kept training, working on her running and her skills. By the time things reopened, she'd grown taller, finally made the team, and her coach told her that if she kept training seriously, she could play at the national level. "That's when I realised I could actually turn this into something," she says.

Starting Five, At Last

Her first real breakthrough came in seventh grade, making her school team as one of the starting five rather than sitting on the bench, years after all her friends had already made it. Basketball, she says, has been behind her closest friendships ever since, in school and now in college, a meaningful gift for someone who describes herself as mostly introverted. Her father, a former cricketer who now plays golf, gets warm credit as the steadiest influence throughout, arranging her training, making sure she got to practice, and being there after every tough loss or missed shot.

By tenth grade she was captain, training more seriously, and trying out for the Haryana state team for the first time, the point she marks as when she truly began believing in herself as an athlete.

The Trial That Wasn't Fair

That belief was tested hard the following year. Living in Gurgaon but attending school in Delhi meant local coaches simply didn't know her, a real disadvantage in these trials. She trained hard, felt good about her tryout, and then learned that the twelve selected players hadn't even shown up to the trial. "I tried not to let it affect me, but it left me feeling hopeless for a while," she says. Her coach noticed and encouraged her to try once more. She tried out for the FIBA 3x3 nationals in Chennai instead, and was selected for that team.

Doubt, Held With Grace

She's honest about having thought, more than once, about stepping away entirely. Sport in India, she points out, offers no guaranteed financial return, only passion, and she's wondered whether that time might be better spent building more employable skills. Even so, she's clear about where she lands. "I don't think I'll ever actually quit," she says.

Her most recent challenge has been physical rather than mental, a knee injury that led to her first real break from basketball in over a year. She's easing back into training now, with a little understandable nervousness about having to try out again once she's back at Flame.

Learning to Show Up

What these setbacks have taught her, more than anything, is that discipline and consistency matter more than talent. She used to assume other players succeeded without putting in much effort. She's since let that idea go. "Becoming better just means showing up and doing the work every single day," she says. Under pressure mid-tournament, she admits she still tends to make more mistakes than she'd like, and calls staying calmer in those moments an ongoing project rather than something she's fully mastered.

Team Over Solitude

Her pre-game routine leans on familiarity rather than intensity: a favourite hype-up playlist, the same warmup every time, and an effort to keep her teammates in a good headspace too, since she believes the team only performs well when everyone's dialled in together. She used to isolate before big matches but has since found it works better to stay close to her teammates, talking about anything unrelated to keep herself calm.

Life Outside the Court

Away from basketball, she's recently picked up golf, reads widely, happily calls herself a foodie always chasing new places to eat, and has been learning Mandarin. Being team captain in school made her feel noticeably different from her peers, a distinction she says she's always appreciated rather than minded.

What She's Building Toward

At Flame University, studying economics with a minor in public policy, she's had her first real stretch of dedicated training time, something she calls the most formative phase of her career so far. She doesn't have a fixed long-term target beyond simply getting better, and perhaps, one day, nationals. Asked to sum herself up in five words, she offers dedicated, somewhat underconfident, still growing, a team player, and, almost as an aside, fast, a nod, in the end, to the very thing she once wasn't.
