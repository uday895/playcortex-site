Sana Talwar waited five years to make a basketball team

"I don't think I'll ever actually quit."

Sana Talwar started basketball in second grade because her friends asked her to stay back for practice. She had always been athletic, and her father, a former cricketer who now plays golf, had always encouraged her to try new sports. What followed was a long run of disappointment. She was short, not especially fast and asthmatic, and from second grade until fifth or sixth grade she did not make a single team, not even at school. "I loved the sport but kept missing out," she says.

Lockdown

Before the Covid lockdown she asked her coach directly why she was not being picked. He told her she was not fast enough yet and needed more skill work. She used the shutdown to fix that, working on her running and her skills despite severe asthma, and despite a Covid infection that reduced her lung capacity further. When things reopened she had grown taller and finally made the team. Her coach told her that if she kept training seriously, she could play at national level. "That's when I realised I could actually turn this into something," she says.

In seventh grade she made the school team as one of the starting five rather than a substitute, years after her friends had all made it. By tenth grade she was captain and trying out for the Haryana state team for the first time, which is when she says she began to believe in herself as an athlete. Basketball has also given her her closest friendships, at school and now at college, which matters to someone who describes herself as mostly introverted. Through all of it her father arranged her training, made sure she got to practice and was there after every bad loss or missed shot.

The state trial

The following year tested that belief. She lived in Gurgaon but went to school in Delhi, so the local coaches running the Haryana trials did not know her. She trained hard and felt good about her tryout, then learned that the twelve players selected had not attended the trial at all. "I tried not to let it affect me, but it left me feeling hopeless for a while," she says. Her coach noticed and encouraged her to try again. She tried out for the FIBA 3x3 nationals in Chennai and was selected.

She has thought more than once about stepping away. Sport in India, she points out, offers no guaranteed financial return, and she has wondered whether the time would be better spent on more employable skills. She always arrives at the same answer. "I don't think I'll ever actually quit," she says.

Showing up

The setbacks taught her that discipline and consistency count for more than talent. She used to assume that other players succeeded without much effort, and she has let that idea go. "Becoming better just means showing up and doing the work every single day," she says. Under pressure in tournaments she still makes more mistakes than she would like, and staying calm in those moments is something she is still working on.

Before games she plays a favourite playlist, does the same warm up every time and tries to keep her teammates in a good frame of mind, because she believes a team only performs when everyone is settled. She used to keep to herself before big matches. Now she stays close to her teammates and talks about anything other than basketball to keep calm.

Away from the court she has taken up golf, reads widely, calls herself a foodie always looking for new places to eat, and has been learning Mandarin.

Flame University

She is studying economics with a minor in public policy at Flame University, where she has had her first long stretch of dedicated training time, and she considers it the most formative period of her career so far. Her latest setback has been physical: a knee injury that brought her first real break from basketball in over a year. She is easing back into training, a little nervous about having to try out again when she returns to Flame.

She has no fixed long term target beyond getting better, and perhaps one day playing at nationals. Asked to describe herself in five words, she chose dedicated, somewhat underconfident, still growing, a team player, and fast.
