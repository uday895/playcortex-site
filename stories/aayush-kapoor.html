Aayush Kapoor

From National Skating Champion to Badminton's Top 15

"Hardworking. Resilient. Eventful."

Aayush Kapoor broke into India's top 15 junior badminton players and has played international tournaments from Hyderabad to Southeast Asia. His story started in a different sport.

Sixteen-Time Delhi Champion

He started skating at four. By his early teens he had won sixteen Delhi State Championships, and from 2013 to 2015 he was national road skating champion three years running.

Then a fall broke both bones in his forearm near the elbow. Doctors warned that one more fall could leave his left hand useless. He had to give up the sport he had mastered.

Starting Over

Badminton came in quietly, through casual rallies with his father. His grandfather had once played internationally. On 3 January 2017, Aayush decided to take the sport seriously.

The first eighteen months were basics: footwork, repetition and small local tournaments. In 2019 he won the Under-13 singles at the district championships in Mayur Vihar and finished second in doubles with his brother. It was his first trophy in his new sport.

A Net in the Society

He played his first All India tournament in Guwahati in 2020, and then Covid stopped everything. He put up a net in his housing society and trained there every morning and every evening.

When competition came back, so did results. At his first badminton Nationals, the All India Junior Championships in Goa in 2022, he reached the quarterfinals and broke into India's top 15 juniors. In August 2023 he played the India Junior International Series at the Pullela Gopichand Academy and later qualified for the Junior International Grand Prix.

3:30 AM, Then Class

He started at SRCC in 2023, waking at 3:30 AM, training at 4 and going straight to class. Between 2023 and 2025 he lost a run of close matches and his confidence dropped. Then a disc bulge at L4-L5, together with scoliosis, began pressing on the nerves in his spine and sent pain down his leg. His last tournament before rehab was the Khelo India University Games, representing Delhi University.

His fight now is with recovery and patience, not an opponent. He has already rebuilt himself once, going from national skating champion to one of India's top junior badminton players.
