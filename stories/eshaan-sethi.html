Eshaan Sethi

From the Fairway to Founding a Company

"I feel being an athlete has given me the confidence to actually start a company of my own."

Eshaan Sethi started playing golf at four, tagging along when his parents played weekend rounds at the Delhi Golf Club. He played cricket and football too, but by ten or eleven he was ranked second or third in the country in his age group. From 12 to 17 he was in the top five on the Indian National Junior Tour and on track to turn professional.

Pinehurst at Ten

When he was ten, his parents took him to the US Kids World Championship in Pinehurst. The other kids were bigger and hit the ball further. He finished around 55th out of 200 and was happy with that. When he came home, he won a few national events. "That's when I realized that yeah maybe I do have it in me."

The Hardest Call

At 17, with Division 1 and Division 3 offers from American colleges, he chose a school with an acceptance rate below 8% over a stronger golf programme. "I kind of used my golf to get into a good academic program." He still isn't sure it was the right call. "It was one of the most difficult decisions I've taken."

Money was part of it. In a team sport the franchise pays for travel and hotels. In golf, he points out, "you're a freelancer, you have to pay for everything," and in a country with few sponsors for sports other than cricket, tournament winnings mostly go on the cost of playing.

At 16, just after his tenth-grade boards, he came back from two or three months away and trained too hard. A tissue tear in his lower back kept him out for five or six months. "Every time you have an injury, it's like a learning lesson for you."

Same Seat, Same Putts

In college golf, his routine never changed: the same seat at the back left of the team van, foam rolling, a set number of balls on the range, then a putting drill with two balls three feet from the hole until he had seen the ball drop again and again. "I was extremely superstitious when I used to play."

A Second Career in Sport

After an economics degree and a year at KPMG, he co-founded a counselling company that helps Indian athletes get recruited into US college sport. It began as a side project and now works with around a dozen athletes, mostly golfers. Golf taught him to focus on the process, one shot at a time, and he runs the business the same way.

He strongly advises young athletes against dropping out of school or switching to open schooling at 13 or 14, and believes every athlete needs a plan B. "No matter how hard you work, if you're not thinking on your feet in the moment, things can go anywhere."
