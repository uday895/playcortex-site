Arpit Tevatia

The midfielder who never settles for less

"If I'm learning something, I want to learn it at a very highest level. Just do it or nothing."

Arpit Tevatia is a midfielder who has played for his college team and several Delhi football clubs, and who once trained at a national camp alongside under-23 players while still a teenager. Right now, the SRCC student is taking a break from professional football to focus on his studies and his first job. But listen to him talk about the game and it is clear the story is not finished.

From KV Shalimar Bagh to the big boys

Arpit started playing in the sixth or seventh standard at KV Shalimar Bagh, kicking a ball around with friends until a coach invited him to a trial for the regional team. He was selected, and that was his first real match. Coming from a police family background, he later trialled for a programme run by the Family Welfare Society that supports young athletes. He was suffering from lower back pain during the trial, gave everything he had anyway, and made the team.

By 14 or 15, he was playing with the Delhi Police senior team, holding his own against grown men. That, he says, is where he improved the most. He then moved to Modern School, the same school Sunil Chhetri attended, where he played zonals and interzonals and gave trials for national teams. He calls that period the peak of his career.

Football as a way into SRCC

A state champion with a sports certificate, Arpit was eligible for university admission through the sports quota. He remembers playing on the ground the day before his entrance exam, confident that his football would carry him. On the advice of seniors and his coach, he put SRCC first on his preference list, and in 2023 he got in, something he says he never even dreamed of. He played in SRCC's starting eleven throughout his time there, never missing crucial matches even when injured, and as a first-year was one of only three freshers to break into the first team.

The injury that made him cry

His biggest setback has been his body. At 17 or 18, at a national camp with under-23 players, he suffered a lower back injury so bad that he had to stop mid-run, hand on his back, and walk. It struck during his final selection dates, just as he was in line to represent Delhi. He admits he cried and blamed God. Over time, he came to a quieter view: some things are never meant to be for you, and you have to accept them. A persistent knee muscle pain followed at college, affecting his shooting and change of direction, and despite physios and scans no clear cause has been found.

Part of the problem, he believes, was the routine he followed in Class 11. He would wake at five for a two-hour bus ride to school, train in the afternoon heat at full intensity, travel home, go to the gym, then play again in the evening, with little food and no guidance. He laughs about it now as a fun, formative time, but he knows it contributed to his injuries.

The Ronaldo mindset, without the ego

A Manchester United fan who keeps a Cristiano Ronaldo wallpaper on his phone, Arpit admires Ronaldo's mentality and discipline. When setbacks come, he tells himself he is good, that he can do it, that he is the best. But he is careful to separate self-belief from ego. He describes himself as a lifelong learner who goes to coaches after trials to ask what he can improve, and who accepts when he is wrong. Early on, he felt intense anxiety at trials, sitting for a whole day waiting for his chance. With experience and match time, that anxiety faded.

His parents, he says, have always been calm and never interfered, letting him make his own decisions, something he considers himself lucky to have had.

What comes next

Arpit managed his own nutrition growing up, adding eggs and whey protein to a basic Indian diet, and he believes young athletes at under-15 and under-18 level need professional nutrition guidance as they grow. His short-term plan is to get back into good form. Longer term, he wants a stable job, fitness that is up to the mark, and a CFA alongside his work, with the CAT after some experience. Above all, he says, he wants to enjoy life. Given how he approaches everything, he will probably do that at the highest level too.
