Samarth Seth

Fifteen years in, still chasing the cap

"It's actually all in the mind. Whatever you think that happens. So if you have control over your mind you can achieve everything."

Samarth Seth, known to most simply as Sam, has been playing cricket for 15 years and first-class cricket since 2018. The Delhi batter has scored hundreds in three countries, reinvented himself from a fast bowler into an opener, and rebuilt his body in his mid-twenties to keep pace with teenagers. As the Delhi Premier League approached, he was preparing as the retained player for Purani Delhi, where he had been the team's highest run-scorer the season before.

A father, a tennis ball and an academy

Like so many Indian cricketers, Sam began with a tennis ball, playing with his dad. His father took him to an academy at around nine, in roughly 2010, and kept taking him everywhere after that, to matches, to academies and to practice sessions before school. Sam says his family has always been very supportive, pushing him to pursue his dream. Was it his father's dream or his own? Both, he says. His father saw something in him first, and later Sam felt it too.

He started as a fast bowler and represented Delhi in the under-14s in 2012. A back injury forced him to give up bowling, and he turned to batting, becoming an opener. In 2014 he played for Delhi in the under-16s as a batsman, and in a match against Jammu, with his team chasing over 500, he scored around 170 to help win the game. That was the moment he knew he could do this professionally.

Hundreds at home and abroad

After the under-19s, Sam moved to Arunachal Pradesh as a guest player and made his debut in 2018, scoring a hundred on debut. Returning to Delhi in 2021, opportunities were scarce in a big, settled setup, though he played under-23 and under-25 cricket. He then spent seasons abroad as an overseas professional: in the UK, in Sri Lanka, where he struck three back-to-back first-class hundreds, and in Northern Ireland in 2024, where his club won everything. He counts representing Delhi as his biggest achievement as a youngster, and those three hundreds in Sri Lanka as another.

The setback he would change

Asked about a major setback, Sam is thoughtful. He began going to the UK on six-month professional deals at 21, and he now wishes he had delayed it. Being away meant being out of Delhi's domestic circuit, with its many tournaments, for half of every year. Since he stopped playing abroad and committed fully to Delhi, he says, his mindset has completely changed. In this game, two or three good innings can change everything.

He is honest that cricket brings more failures than good days, and that some days the thought of quitting creeps in. But after doing something for so long, he says, you realise you cannot live without it. He has had fractures, a dislocated finger and an operation, and broke a finger before a first-class game. Injuries, he says, are part of the process. They will happen to everyone.

Temple, calm and a new body

In difficult moments, Sam does not need many words. He is deeply spiritual, visiting the temple every second day, sitting quietly until his energy returns. It keeps him sane, he says, in a sport that moves fast and evolves every day. He also speaks openly about mental health, arguing that a mental breakdown is normal in a high-intensity sport watched by so many, even if almost no one in India talks about it.

For more than two years he has worked with a strength and conditioning coach, losing 10 to 12 kilograms. He turned vegetarian three years ago and now eats almost the same meals every day, including the soya and paneer tikki his mother makes. He wishes he had known all this at 16. If his mindset and body had been what they are now, he believes he would have reached his goals sooner.

Calm off the field, ambitious on it

Away from cricket, Sam calls himself calm, composed and happy in a small circle. He loves music, travel, suits, watches and shoes, and dreams of seeing the Northern Lights, skydiving and exploring Europe while he is young. One constant through it all has been his coach, with him since age 13, who turned him from a bowler into a batsman. His short-term goal is to be the best player in the DPL and push for an IPL contract. His long-term dream has never changed: to play for India, and to stay there at the highest level.
