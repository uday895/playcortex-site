Kashish Pansari

The Girl Who Was Scared of the Ball

"I was very scared of the ball hitting my face."

Kashish Pansari has played football at district, zonal and inter-college level, but she started out afraid of the ball.

In fourth grade she moved to Mumbai and a new school, where everyone had to pick an after-school activity. "All the cool kids were going for football," so she chose it too. The problem was that she was terrified of the ball hitting her face.

The Wall

Two teammates came up with a fix. One held her against a wall while the other kept throwing the ball at her face, on purpose. "Yes, yeah, it's a real story." At the time it was horrifying. Looking back, she says, "That was very much needed." The fear went away, and she started to love the game.

So Close to State

She played inter-school and district competitions, then zonals. Her team qualified for the next round, close to representing Maharashtra at state level. Then came the Diwali holidays. Teammates were out of town, the team couldn't travel, and the chance was gone. "It was a very sad moment."

Her school coach later started Kopana Football School nearby. For a long time she was the only girl there. She kept training, her friends gradually joined, and eventually there was a separate girls' team.

Every Position on the Pitch

For years she played in goal because the team needed a keeper and she wanted a place in the eleven. Then a Mumbai newspaper printed her photo and singled out her goalkeeping. "That's when it grew on me." She liked how much keepers matter, even though people rarely celebrate them.

When she changed schools in eighth grade, the team already had a keeper, so she asked for her own trial and was put in as a defensive midfielder. In eleventh and twelfth, most of her team moved to the IB section and she was "low-key, the only person remaining." At Lady Shri Ram College she joined a young team that finished fourth at Inter-DU and won a major college tournament. She plays in defence now.

The Inner Child

She has played through a minor ligament tear and dealt with a referee's decision that cost her school team qualification. In sixth grade, like most kids, she was obsessed with Messi and dreamed of going pro. That faded as other interests grew. She has now released her first song and is working towards a career as an independent musician.

She will keep playing, though, probably with a weekend club after college. Her younger brother has caught the same football bug, which she calls a "full circle moment."

"Playing the sport keeps the inner child inside me alive."
