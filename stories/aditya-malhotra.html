Aditya Malhotra

One Ball on Live TV, and a Year of Motivation

"Now that I think about it, I feel like I started working harder after that."

Aditya Malhotra started playing cricket at four or five, right after the 2011 World Cup, when the whole country was caught up in the game. Back then he had no idea what the pathway to playing for India looked like. He just knew he was one of the better players at school, and by six or seven he wanted to make a career of it.

Academies, Coaches and a Cancelled Season

He moved between academies often and trained under six or seven coaches, and he credits all of them. For the last two years he has been with Mr. Ajit and Mr. Sanjay. Ajit sir pushed him into senior tournaments where Aditya didn't expect to get a game.

He was picked for Delhi's Under-16 squad in 2022-23, but the tournament was cancelled during the third Covid wave. In the 50-over trials later on he scored two centuries in the first round, then two unbeaten fifties in the final round. In August 2025 he was picked for the DPL, and in October he made Delhi's top 20 for the Under-19 Vinoo Mankad Trophy.

A Yorker at 140

By the time he got a DPL game, his team was already out of the tournament. He walked out on live TV to face Simarjeet Singh. The first ball was a wide, and then the bowler took two or three minutes to fix his run-up. "I felt like it was like 10, 15, 20 minutes." That was when it hit him that thousands of people were watching. The next ball was a 140 kmph yorker, and he was bowled.

It hurt to get out first ball after waiting so long for the chance. "Now that I think about it, I feel like I started working harder after that."

Taking a Step Back

After the Under-19 season, where he was among the leading run-getters but didn't get a debut, he felt disconnected from the game. He took two or three weeks off and travelled. "If I played during that time, I just would have started hating this game." He still wants teammates who get picked ahead of him to do well.

He used to bowl fast, but his father, who knew the injury risk and the family history of back problems, had him stop at nine or ten. He is now a batting all-rounder. He plays in the middle order but prefers to open, because the new ball and the powerplay are his favourite part of the game.

The Road Ahead

He grew up idolising Virat Kohli and admires Ishan Kishan's comeback. In the short term he wants to do well in the DPL and get called to IPL trials. The long-term dream hasn't changed since he was four: to play for India.

Disciplined, consistent and determined are the words he agreed with when asked to describe himself.
