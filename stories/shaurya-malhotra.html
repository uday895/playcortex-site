Shaurya Malhotra

Finding Flow in the Chaos

"The calmer you are, the more clearly you can think."

Shaurya Malhotra came to badminton after golf, tennis and squash, all sports his father introduced him to. Around the age of ten, badminton clicked. "It requires speed and reflexes, so it was my type of game." His father woke him at 5 AM for runs and casual games, and the early starts became a lifelong habit.

Losing First

He lost in the first round of his very first tournament after a full year of practice. "From the start, I just didn't want to lose, and that kept me going." Two years in, he reached the doubles final and the singles semi-final at an Under-15 district tournament, and decided to stay with the sport.

For years he trained six to eight hours a day. He was up at 4:15, trained at 5, caught the school bus at 8, trained again in the evening and was asleep by 10:30. He trained for a month at Thailand's national camp and spent six months at Prakash Padukone's academy in Bangalore. He became the Under-19 fourth seed in Uttar Pradesh and won state bronze in singles.

So Close, Twice

In his last Under-19 season, he lost in the final qualifying round at two All India tournaments back to back, in Jaipur and Bangalore. He put the racket down for a month or two and felt even more lost. "It had become such a deep habit." Then he went back, and he hasn't stopped since.

Senior men's badminton brought bigger opponents and injuries to his back, shoulder and ankle. Last year he twisted his ankle in the first set of an All India qualifier, played the set out and lost, then spent two weeks in bed thinking about quitting. "You've made your life badminton. You can't live without it."

Learning Calm

As a younger player he was very hard on himself and often cried after losses. Now he aims for calm at the big points. "What's done is done." Before matches he uses box breathing. He keeps a diary and plans his days hour by hour. He studies at Shiv Nadar University, where he now trains about five hours a day: "but those five hours are quality hours."

His idol is Lee Chong Wei, who was so often the runner-up. Shaurya's goal is to build an All India ranking and move on to international events. His advice to his younger self: "Don't be so hard on yourself."
