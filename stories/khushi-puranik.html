Khushi Puranik

Finding Happiness Inside Four Walls

"The love I had for the sound of the ball, the movement on court, I think that stayed."

Khushi Puranik's father is in the Air Force, and the family had one rule: every new posting meant trying something new. She earned a brown belt in karate, skated and played tennis for four years in Chandigarh. In Delhi she started hitting with her father on the squash court, and he saw something in the way she struck the ball.

Blacking Out

They signed her up for an Under-13 tournament, expecting her to beat everyone. Instead, she froze. "All I remember is stepping onto the court and blacking out completely." It happened in her first three tournaments. Her father suggested meditation to steady her breathing, and after that she stopped freezing. Her confidence off the court grew too.

The real turning point came when a stranger at the DDA Sports Complex, someone who worked in the corporate world rather than as a coach, watched her play and offered to coach her for free. "A complete stranger putting belief in me like that, I think that was my wake-up moment."

Rankings and a Ligament

She reached number 8 in India in Girls Under-19, number 12 in the women's category and 243 in the world. She played for Delhi at the SGFI Games and won a full sports scholarship to Shiv Nadar University. On the day in 2023 she learned she was in the top eight, which meant a shot at the national team trials, she found herself with a torn ligament. It happened at a Diwali party, dancing in Crocs. "I felt very stupid," she says, laughing about it now.

A Fighter's Title

As training became a requirement of the scholarship, her love for the game faded. "My name is Khushi, meaning happiness, and I used to be genuinely happy inside the squash court." Her self-worth had become tied to her results, and she went through a very difficult stretch. In the middle of it, she won her first national title in a five-game final. "I felt like I'd crossed such a big hurdle in my life."

In her final tournament, in Bangalore, she came back from 2-0 down and lost the decider 11-9. "I felt like a true fighter." In tight moments she makes herself laugh: "What are you doing, silly," and then on to the next point.

After the Racket

She has stepped away from competitive squash and now lives in Nagpur. She plans to coach kids cheaply, because the sport is so expensive, and to study sports management. She credits her whole family: her mother drove her and planned her meals, her father managed her career, and her younger sister gave up her drama club for tournament travel. "Once an athlete, always an athlete."
