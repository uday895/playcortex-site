Harsh Bhardwaj lost 60 kilograms to chase a career in cricket

"Focus. Focus."

Harsh Bhardwaj once weighed 125 kilograms. He now weighs around 65. The Delhi cricketer, recently selected in the East Delhi Riders squad for the Delhi Premier League, lost that weight during the lockdown of 2020 and 2021, when he decided to commit to cricket as a profession.

A late start with a leather ball

Cricket had his attention long before it became serious. He traces it back to Classes 8 and 9, when school house competitions and sport took over most of his time. His first games with a leather ball came in 2018 and 2019, through school trials, while he was also getting through Class 12.

The lockdown was the turning point. He took on a strict regime of training, dieting and batting practice, and brought his weight down from 125 kilograms to about 65.

Trials, T20s and the DDCA

He spent the years from 2021 to 2023 practising and playing trial matches, including Under 23 trials and games. He was still finding his footing in 2023 and felt comfortable with his cricket by 2024. He counts his selection with the DDCA, the Delhi and District Cricket Association, as his first big achievement. In the 2022 T20 season he finished among the top eight bowlers, usually as his team's fourth bowling option.

Pushing too hard

The 2025 season was harder. Harsh describes a period of overexertive training without proper guidance, which wore him down physically and mentally. He also had a spine injury. He kept playing all the same, and appeared in 15 T20 matches for his club that season.

He believes better guidance would have made a difference. His workload and training are now overseen by a doctor. He wants a strict strength and conditioning schedule, and is working in particular on his quads and hamstrings. Asked how he would spend unlimited resources to help athletes, he put cricket first, along with proper support staff: nutritionists, psychologists, dieticians and recovery coaches.

Family and focus

Through good times and difficult ones, he says, his mother and father have given him 100 percent support. His mother looks after his diet, making sure he gets his protein and his meals at the right times. He values that backing far more than the opinions of extended relatives.

Cricket takes up most of his life. He has often turned down invitations, weddings included, rather than miss a training or bowling session, and he accepts that people sometimes misunderstand him for it. His Instagram feed is mostly cricket too. He calls the athlete community around him "one of the best", with players constantly motivating each other.

His role model is Virat Kohli, for his aggression and discipline. What gets him going each day, he says, is discipline and keeping to a consistent schedule at his academy. Asked what he would tell his younger self, he kept it short: "Focus. Focus."

His short term goal is to keep that focus every day. His long term goal is the Indian Premier League, and when asked which franchise, he named Royal Challengers Bangalore.
