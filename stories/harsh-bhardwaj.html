Harsh Bhardwaj

Sixty kilos lighter, eyes on the IPL

"My name is Harsh Bhardwaj and I'm a professional cricketer and currently selected in DPL East Rider Delhi team."

There is a version of Harsh Bhardwaj's story that begins on a cricket field, and another that begins on a weighing scale. Both are true. The Delhi cricketer, recently picked for the East Delhi Riders squad in the Delhi Premier League, once weighed 125 kilograms. Today he is around 65. The distance between those two numbers is the clearest measure of how much he has been willing to give to the game.

A late start with a leather ball

Harsh's journey into serious cricket began around 2018 and 2019, through school trials where he first played with a leather ball. He was in his final years of school at the time, balancing the demands of Class 12 with a growing pull towards the nets. Cricket had already captured his heart well before that. He traces his devotion back to the 8th and 9th grade, when school house competitions and sport began to take up most of his attention.

The lockdown that changed everything

The real turning point came during the 2020 and 2021 lockdown. While much of the world stood still, Harsh decided that this was the moment to commit to cricket professionally. That decision meant a complete overhaul of his life: a rigorous regime of training, careful dieting and long hours of batting and bowling practice. The result was a transformation few people manage in any walk of life, let alone as a teenager chasing a sport. He brought his weight down from 125 kilograms to roughly 65, an achievement his interviewer described as critical for his development as an athlete.

The years from 2021 to 2023 were about building on that foundation. Harsh describes them as a period of steady practice and trial matches, including Under-23 trials and games. He was still finding his footing in 2023, but by 2024 he felt comfortable with where his cricket was heading. Along the way he counts his selection with the DDCA, the Delhi and District Cricket Association, as his first big achievement, and he remembers the 2022 T20 season fondly, when he finished among the top eight bowlers while usually serving as his team's fourth bowling option.

Pushing too hard

Not every chapter has been smooth. In 2025, Harsh went through what he calls a period of overexertive training. Without the right guidance, he pushed his body beyond what it could sustain, and the strain showed up both physically and mentally. He also dealt with a spine injury. Even so, he kept turning up, playing 15 T20 matches for his cricket club that season.

Looking back, he is clear about the lesson. Better guidance would have made a difference. Today his workload and training are overseen by a doctor, and he speaks with conviction about the need for nutritionists, psychologists, dieticians and recovery coaches around every young athlete. Strength and conditioning, he says, needs a strict schedule, and he is working specifically on building his quads and hamstrings.

Family first, always

Through the good times and the difficult ones, Harsh says his mother and father have given him 100 percent of their support. His mother plays a quiet but crucial role in his preparation, managing his protein intake and making sure his meals arrive at the right times. He values that family backing far more than the opinions of extended relatives, a quality his interviewer said they both share.

That single-mindedness has a cost. Harsh has often turned down social invitations, weddings included, so that he does not miss a training or bowling session. Sometimes people misunderstand him for it. He accepts that. Even his Instagram feed, he admits with a smile, is mostly cricket.

Discipline and a dream in red

His role model is Virat Kohli, and it is Kohli's aggression and discipline that Harsh tries to carry into his own game. Asked what gets him out of bed every day, his answer is simple: discipline, and a commitment to a consistent schedule at his training academy. If he could send one message to his younger self, it would be just as short. Focus. Focus.

His short-term goal is to keep that focus every single day. His long-term goal is the Indian Premier League, and he already knows which shirt he wants to wear. Asked which franchise he dreams of joining, he did not hesitate: Royal Challengers Bangalore. For a cricketer who has already rebuilt himself once, it does not feel like an unreasonable ambition.
