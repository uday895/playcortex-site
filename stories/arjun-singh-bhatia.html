Arjun Singh Bhatia

Chasing the Green Jacket

"If I keep knocking on that door, someone's going to open it eventually."

Before Arjun Singh Bhatia held a golf club, he was swinging a little stick made to copy his father's horse polo. His grandfather, his Nanu, saw the action and said, "Isko golf shuru karao." His father, an army officer with golf courses always close by, took the advice. Arjun first held a club at five. His first real memories of the game are from Jodhpur at nine or ten, playing evening rounds with his father and his father's colleagues.

From Fun to Focus

For years it was just fun. In 2017 he finished second at the North Zone event, qualified for the national circuit and had his first lesson with Jesse Grewal, the coach he still works with. He struggled in his first year on the IGU Main Tour but was finishing in the top three by the end of 2019. During Covid he changed his body and the way he practised, and he decided to take golf seriously.

In 2021 he won the national amateur qualifier, beating future India number one Shaurya Bhattacharya in the final round. In 2023 he became India's number one in the Under-18 category.

A Big Fish in Open Water

He arrived at Long Island University ranked number one in India by about 80 points. His freshman year was his worst in five years. Unfamiliar greens pushed his scoring average up by about two strokes, and he had to manage life on his own for the first time, from meals to laundry. He says college made him more mature off the course than on it.

He went two and a half years without a college win. "I'd tell myself I'm just going to keep showing up, keep giving myself these chances." With help from his sports psychologist, the win came in April this year.

The Kid With the Polo Stick

He credits his father, who was at every lesson and every tournament; his mother, a former national-level hockey player who drove him to practice every day; and his college coach, Ben, for some tough love. He is now moving to the University of North Texas, a top-50 programme. His goals are to turn pro, play the PGA Tour and win the Masters.

"That kid in a photo holding a polo stick, that's why I play golf." Five words for himself: persistent, committed, arrogant, competitive, passionate.
