Krrish Vikram Jain

Back on the pitch in five months

"I had a goal in mind that I need to get back to my 100% level. I think that really kept me going."

Krrish Vikram Jain was 10 years old when he first played against under-16 boys, 12 when he represented India in a club tournament in Barcelona, and a university freshman when a complete ACL tear threatened to stop everything. Today he is vice-captain of the Ashoka University football team, a centre back expected to take the captain's armband next, and proof that a serious athlete can also be a serious student.

A first touch in America

Like most Indian kids, Krrish started with cricket. Then, around 2014 or 2015, on a family trip to the US, he played football with his older brother and something clicked. By 2016 he had started formal coaching at school, playing in midfield and even keeping goal for penalties. When he started training properly, he says, he realised this was the sport for him.

That same year he joined Bhaichung Bhutia Football Schools, one of Delhi's best known grassroots academies. Within his first month, at 10, the coaches had him playing with the under-16s. The exposure to competitive football was invaluable, and in 2017 he was picked for the Delhi development squad.

Barcelona and Portugal

In 2017, at 12, Krrish was selected to travel to Barcelona with BBFS for a club tournament. Playing as a striker, he finished as the tournament's third top scorer and his team came third. The next year he went to Portugal to train and play league and beach football, where he caught the eye of European scouts. He chose not to stay, with family and education in mind. He reflects that if he had stayed and not made it, he might have been left with nothing to fall back on.

In 2019, at 13, he made the Delhi under-15 team through open trials, though the pandemic stopped play. BBFS kept its players going with online sessions on fitness and ball mastery in their own rooms.

Captain at school, champion in the cluster

Back at school, Krrish captained his team to the CBSE cluster championship in Delhi, beating around 140 to 150 schools, and then played the CBSE nationals in Rajasthan. In Class 12, in 2024, his team won the zonal and interzonal titles and he was selected for the Delhi state school games team as a centre back, but after talking with his parents he chose to focus on his board exams. He scored well and got into good universities. Football, he believes, helped. Colleges value students who can balance sport and academics.

He credits the game with teaching him perseverance, discipline and leadership. He has captained his school and a Delhi side. His parents always encouraged him to enjoy his experiences abroad rather than pressuring him, and his close friends still tell him he could play professionally.

The ACL and the mental battle

On 21 September 2024, during a college match, Krrish suffered a complete ACL tear. It was the first injury of his life, and the worst kind. He had surgery on 10 October. What followed, he says, was a huge mental battle, with people around him wondering whether he would ever get back to his level. He kept his head down, did physio every day and held on to one goal: getting back to 100 percent.

He was back training with his university team on 15 March 2025, about five months after surgery, and two weeks later walked into the starting eleven at a major college sports fest, which his team won. He says it was the first time in his college's history a freshman started in his first tournament. That season, his team won six of eight tournaments.

Finance, family and giving back

Krrish has always processed bad games internally, replaying what he could have done differently, and has always treated his coaches with deep respect. His advice to young players is to follow a consistent weekly strength and conditioning plan reviewed by a coach, and to treat nutrition as just as important as training.

Off the pitch, he is a finance enthusiast who has followed markets since 16, has travelled to more than 20 countries, and is preparing for the GMAT and a postgraduate degree in finance. His idols are Cristiano Ronaldo for his work ethic and Sergio Ramos for his leadership. With unlimited resources, he would build a state-of-the-art football training facility in India to give back to the sport. Closer to home, he wants his parents to enjoy a comfortable retirement.
