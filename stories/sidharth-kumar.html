Sidharth Kumar

Sidharth Kumar, the Delhi Fast Bowler Learning to Let Bad Days Go

Sidharth Kumar's father played Ranji Trophy cricket, but the choice to play was Sidharth's own. He started at nine at Frank Anthony School out of his own interest, and he decided early that he would become a professional fast bowler. He went on to represent Delhi at Under 16 and Under 19 level.

His early progress came mostly from his own drive. He reached the Under 14 Nationals on the strength of his own motivation and discipline. Before he joined Akash Sudan's setup, he felt that coaches had already put a label on him and decided what kind of player he was, and that affected his confidence and how supported he felt.

The Under 16 season

His Under 16 season with Delhi was the hardest of his early career. From the first match, against Hyderabad, he felt he was not being treated fairly in selection and opportunity. During the same tournament he picked up a strain and was dropped for two matches. The pain, the lack of backing and the uncertainty together wore down his motivation, and his form stalled.

He then had to choose between playing through the injury and sitting out. Against Bihar he sat out, a decision he later regretted. A poor performance in the quarterfinal ended the tournament, and he went into what he calls a long "bad phase" in which his motivation and confidence dropped.

A second injury

After the Under 16 season he began to learn what professional cricket demanded, but he also felt the strain of being an active squad player without much support for the mental side of the game. His motivation held through the Under 19 camp. Then, in his second Under 19 year, a severe strain stopped him again, and he went through a confusing and difficult period.

Repeated setbacks and performances below his own expectations built up self doubt. The negative voice in his head began to shape how he saw himself, and it showed in how he played.

A factors and B factors

In December 2023, at a low point, he met Gavnish bhaiya, who supported him above all as a friend and mentor, and for whom he is deeply grateful. Gavnish and Akash Sudan introduced him to the idea of A factors, the things he could control, and B factors, the things he could not. When the A factors are handled well, he learned, the B factors lose much of their power.

He began to notice two states in himself. His conscious mind was busy, negative and fixed on detail. The other, quieter state was where he played freely. Learning to move between them became daily work. The biggest change came when he deliberately separated his identity from cricket. That eased the conflict and brought him some peace. By 2025, with Akash bhaiya's continued guidance, he had stopped worrying about other people's opinions and started enjoying the work.

Back in Delhi colours

He had not played for nearly two years when he was picked for the Delhi Under 19 one day side in 2025. He performed well under pressure on debut. In the second match, against Punjab, he struggled, but this time it did not define him. Through the difficult stretch that followed, Sagar bhaiya, Akash Sudan's brother, told him not to dwell on bad days and to move on like a professional, focusing on the positives. The head coach, Raju Sharma, backed him too. He went on to perform well through the next season.

Looking ahead

He is now moving towards senior cricket, and he sees the game as one part of a larger life. He is grateful to his father, who always treated failure and bad days as normal and stood by him throughout, and to the Sudan family. He still remembers calling Akash bhaiya after a tough match as an Under 14 player, and how much calmer he felt after talking to him.

He describes himself as a fighter, funny and flamboyant. Nothing is guaranteed in cricket, he says, least of all at senior level. What he works on now is getting through bad days, protecting his headspace and enjoying the process.
