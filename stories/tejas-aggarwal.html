Tejas Aggarwal

The leg-spinner who chose to coach

"I only had one thing I always feel in me: the hunger, determination, hardworking ability and loyalty, honesty towards whatever I do."

Some careers in cricket are measured in runs and wickets. Tejas Aggarwal's is measured in the players he helps along the way. Today he is a professional cricket coach with All-Art Academy in Mumbai, works with the UP Warriors in the Women's Premier League and serves as a talent scout for the Kolkata Knight Riders. He got there by being honest with himself about where his own playing career was heading, and by deciding to give back to the game anyway.

Gloves, then a googly

Tejas started playing seriously at 16 in 2011, which he admits is a little late, at Mumbai's Azad Maidan, like so many city kids before him. Inspired by MS Dhoni and Adam Gilchrist, he began as a wicket-keeper batter. But in a practice match crowded with keepers, a coach asked if he could do anything else. A lifelong Shane Warne fan, he said he bowled leg-spin. He had never really tried. His first over was not brilliant, but the ball spun naturally out of his hand, and his interest in spin grew from there.

Across the road stood the historic Bombay Gymkhana, the ground every Azad Maidan kid dreamed of practising on. One day, after sitting out a match in tears, he noticed crowds of children heading there for selection trials. He joined in, made it through the week, and earned a full season of practice at the Gymkhana. He went on to attend under-16 and under-19 camps and played alongside a batch that included future IPL cricketers, even though he never made the Mumbai team himself.

The honest conversation

At around 20, Tejas was introduced to Abhishek Nayar, who observed him for a week and then took him into his academy. There he bowled to stars such as Ajinkya Rahane, Rohit Sharma, Robin Uthappa and Shreyas Iyer, and learned what it really takes to become an India or IPL cricketer. Eventually Nayar had a frank conversation with him: the gap between his skill set and the highest level was big, and it was time to think about a different path. He suggested strength and conditioning or cricket coaching.

Tejas chose coaching without hesitation. Cricket, he says, gave him his good habits and changed him as a person, and he felt he had something to give back. His coaching career began in mid-2019, first as what he calls a paid internship under Nayar.

Learning to coach from scratch

He quickly realised that playing and coaching are completely different crafts. He read cricket books, took courses, studied video and worked on the ground from 7 in the morning to 8 or 9 at night, taking roughly one day off a month. Starting at 23, he says, gave him the liberty to fail. In 2020 and 2021 he co-founded Divine Cricket Academy with coach Ajinkya Kamlay, learning the soft skills of the job, before choosing to join All-Art Academy in 2023 so he could keep upgrading as the game evolved.

The work has taken him to unexpected places, including two and a half years as an assistant coach on the film Mr. and Mrs. Mahi, helping Janhvi Kapoor and Rajkummar Rao play like cricketers. The hours he watched film stars put in changed how he sees their profession. In the T20 Mumbai League, he coached with the Mumbai Falcons, and in the women's team's first season the franchise won the title, his first big win on a big platform.

Fuel from the hard years

Tejas does not hide the difficult parts. As a child he struggled with obesity and faced bullying and body-shaming, and people told him a cricket career was impossible. He turned those voices into fuel. As a coach without elite playing experience, he also faced the question of what he had played, and answered it by studying harder and working day in, day out. His parents first saw cricket as a hobby, but watching his discipline and the change in him, they became his strongest support, and never pressured him about money.

A coach is only as good as his players

His short-term goal is simply to keep getting better so that his students do. His long-term goal is to see as many of them as possible reach the IPL, the Indian team or the Mumbai side, without repeating his mistakes. Given infinite resources, he would build a large institution where underprivileged children can study and play many sports, not just cricket. For Tejas, hundreds of others achieving their dreams is a far bigger dream than achieving his own.
