Manvik Shah

Battle Within

"Your best friend and your best enemy is within you itself."

Manvik Shah first saw basketball on an NBA broadcast as a small child and asked his mother what it was. He was already tall for his age, so his parents signed him up for evening training at a nearby school.

From Hobby to Nationals

At first it was just an after-school activity. Then a local coach started sessions nearby, his father enrolled him, and he began training at 5 AM on weekends. He made his school team in seventh grade. After his team placed third or fourth at the CISCE regionals, he was picked for the CISCE Nationals. "That was the first nationals of my life," and it showed him how good the competition really was.

He signed up for formal coaching just before Covid, then went two weeks before everything shut. After the lockdown he joined a club, and the standard there opened his eyes. "Now I could see good players, like really good players." With better coaching his game improved quickly.

Setbacks Off and On Court

His first obstacle was at home. He comes from a vegetarian family where gyms and whey protein were unfamiliar, and it took a long time to convince his parents. He ran a lot, but without the right diet his fitness lagged.

Last year, after his boards, he finally started training in a gym, eating well and seeing his game improve. Then he tore an ankle ligament in the second quarter of an important tournament. It happened the day before his 18th birthday, which he spent in hospital getting an MRI. Delhi University trials were a week later, when he had only just learned to walk again. His parents backed him, and he got through.

Sidelined in Delhi

He moved to Delhi from a state where basketball isn't a big sport and found himself among the best players in the city. Coming back from injury, he barely got any time on court, even in practice games. He accepts it was fair and calls it a double-edged sword: it knocked his confidence, but it also raised his standards.

By his own estimate he has lost 60-70% of the matches he has played. The joy of the wins keeps him going. "You could be sad about it, but you shouldn't completely stop working hard."

What Basketball Built

He says basketball shaped his whole personality: hard work, not giving up, treating opponents as opponents and accepting every teammate. Balancing early practices, classes, society work, the gym and a social life wears him down, but one good basket makes up for a lot. Away from the game for too long, "I don't feel myself."

His goals now are to get properly fit, fully recover his ankle and get back into his team's top five. After that he wants to earn coaching certifications and become a coach. To his younger self he would say: play your own game, work harder and get fitter sooner.
