Zaid Patankar

Fast bowler, piano student, Mumbai probable

"Whenever I step onto that field, I cross the line, everything else outside the game just shuts off."

Zaid Patankar chose fast bowling because the queue was shorter. At his first under-14 district selection, still unsure whether he was a batter or a bowler, he saw two lines and the batters' line was much longer. He joined the bowlers. Years later he has represented Mumbai in every age group, played for Mumbai Under-23, spent a season as a specialist bowler with Kolkata Knight Riders, and is now among the Ranji Trophy probables.

A hobby that became a path

Zaid started at six, simply because he wanted to play a sport and loved cricket. Nobody in his family had ever pursued the game as a career. He trained at an academy near his home in Navi Mumbai, and at that first district selection, selectors told him he had real potential. He played just one game that year, but the coaches told his family they could support him in this career. In 8th grade, he moved to Don Bosco Matunga, travelling from Navi Mumbai into the city for better school cricket.

In his first under-16 year, he went to a Mumbai-wide fast bowling talent hunt open to all ages. He was the youngest there, and was picked. That led to the selection tournament and his first breakthrough into the Mumbai under-16 team. The travel, team meetings and camaraderie made him fall even more in love with the game. In his second under-16 season he was a regular starter, and Mumbai won the All India Championship, his first title. He was then picked for the West Zone team, though COVID cancelled the camp.

The low that made him better

His first big setback came in his final under-19 year. As a senior in the age group who had already represented the state, he was expected to play. His performances were not up to the mark, and he was not picked even once all season. Zaid went back to the drawing board, worked out what was and was not working, built routines, and learned to control the controllables. He came back the next year to be selected for Mumbai's under-23 side in his first year in the age group, a big jump, and has played consistently since, reaching the semi-finals in back-to-back seasons.

He has been fortunate with injuries. The only real one was a back spasm during under-16 selection games, which he played through. Aches and cuts, he says, are just part of being a fast bowler.

Dad, the anchor

His parents have backed him from the start, in different ways. His mother, a teacher, found it tough knowing studies would take a hit, and still keeps him at his books. He is in his third year of a BMS degree at Rizvi College in Bandra. His father has been his anchor, talking with him every day about cricket and life and giving him perspective. Among his coaches, he singles out Abhishek Nayar from his time at the KKR academy in Mumbai.

His idols are Dale Steyn for his aggression and consistency, James Anderson for swinging the ball both ways, and Jasprit Bumrah as the best of the modern era. He watches old fast bowling clips on YouTube to learn how they use the crease.

Built for the long haul

Strength and conditioning has been central to his career since a talent hunt camp at 13, starting with body-weight work and foundational strength. During lockdown he set up a home gym. He wanted to be the fittest cricketer around, and he credits that work with keeping him injury-free. As a kid he ate whatever he liked, but through the association's high performance camp he now follows a structured plan: a high-protein breakfast, a carb-heavy pre-practice meal and fruit after training. He has cut out sugar, energy drinks being the only exception.

Rest days and the piano

Outside cricket, Zaid loves routine, waking early and working on his mind and body. On rest days, which he finds hard because he has to fill the time, he practises the piano, still an amateur by his own account. What has never changed is the love of the game and the feeling of taking a wicket, which he calls euphoric. His short-term goals are to break into Mumbai's senior side and win both under-23 trophies. Long term, he wants to play for India, win a World Cup and, if cricket gets its chance, an Olympic gold.
