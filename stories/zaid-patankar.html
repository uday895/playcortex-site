Zaid Patankar

Zaid Patankar, the Mumbai fast bowler who picked the shorter queue

"You'll fail. You'll fail again and again. But then you figure things out yourself."

Zaid Patankar became a fast bowler because the queue was shorter. At his first Under 14 district selection he still did not know whether he was a batter or a bowler. There were two lines, the batters' line was much longer, and so he joined the bowlers. The selectors told him he had real potential. He has since represented Mumbai in every age group, played for Mumbai Under 23, spent a season with Kolkata Knight Riders as a specialist bowler, and is now among the Ranji Trophy probables.

From Navi Mumbai to Matunga

He started at six, at an academy close to home in Navi Mumbai. No one in his family had played cricket as a career. He simply wanted to play a sport, and he loved this one. In that first district season he played only one game, but the coaches told his family they could support him in cricket. The next year he did well enough to reach the selection games, and that was when cricket became the plan. His international school barely had a team, so in eighth grade he moved to Don Bosco in Matunga, travelling into Mumbai for better school cricket.

In his first Under 16 year, Mumbai held a fast bowling talent hunt open to players of all ages. He was the youngest there and was picked. That took him to the state selection tournament, where he performed well and earned his first place in the Mumbai Under 16 squad. He played only two or three games that season, but the travel, the team meetings and the friendships made him love the sport even more. The following season he started every game, and Mumbai won the All India Under 16 championship, still the only title he has won with Mumbai. He was then picked for West Zone, but Covid stopped him going to the camp, and he lost his first Under 19 season as well.

A season on the outside

He played for Mumbai again in his second Under 19 year. In his third and final year he was a senior in the age group, someone who had already represented the state and was expected to play. His performances were not good enough, and he was not picked once all season.

He went back to the drawing board. He worked out what was working and what was not, built routines to perform more consistently, and learned to control what he could control. "During the lows is when you learn more about yourself," he says. The next year he was picked for Mumbai Under 23 in his first season in the age group, a big step because many of those players are one level from the senior side. Mumbai reached the semi finals that year, and again this season, when he played every game.

He also spent a season with KKR as a specialist bowler, learning from IPL players and watching how they prepared for games. Of all his coaches, he singles out Abhishek Nayar, whom he worked with at the KKR academy in Mumbai.

He has been lucky with injuries. The only notable one was a back spasm during the Under 16 selection games, which he played through. Aches, niggles and cuts on his feet, he says, are simply part of being a fast bowler.

Family

His parents backed him from the start, in different ways. His mother is a teacher, and she knew that changing schools for cricket meant his studies would suffer. She still keeps after him to study. He is in the third year of a BMS degree at Rizvi College in Bandra. His father has been his anchor. They talk every day, about cricket, his game and life. "He's been an anchor for me since the start," Zaid says.

The fittest cricketer

As a boy he wanted to be the fittest cricketer around. His father started him on light bodyweight training before he was 13, and at 13, after being picked for a Mumbai talent hunt camp, he first trained in a gym and asked every trainer there for a programme. During the lockdown he built a gym at home. Now he works with a specialist fast bowling trainer on programmes to bowl faster, stay consistent and manage the risk of injury, and he credits that work with keeping him free of serious injuries. Friends in his age group know where to find him, he says: in the gym or training.

Food was not planned when he was young. He ate what children eat, and sometimes did not eat before a game at all. Through the Mumbai Cricket Association's high performance camp he now has a plan drawn up by nutritionists: a high protein breakfast, a pre practice meal heavy in carbohydrates, and fruit after training. He has given up sugar, with energy drinks the one exception.

His idols are Dale Steyn, for his aggression and consistency; James Anderson, for swinging the ball both ways; and Jasprit Bumrah, the best of the modern era. He watches old fast bowling clips on YouTube to see how they used the crease.

Wickets and the piano

Outside cricket he enjoys routine: waking early, looking after his body and mind, and learning new skills. He plays the piano, though he calls himself an amateur. Rest days are hard for him because he has to find something to do, so that is when he practises most, and on busy days he plays a little before bed to wind down. He loves touring as well: the long bus journeys, the music, and weeks away with teammates who, over a season, become a second family.

What has stayed constant is the love of the game, and the slow work of getting better at it. "You'll fail. You'll fail again and again. But then you figure things out yourself," he says. Then there is the feeling of beating a batter and taking a wicket, which he calls euphoric. His goals for this season are to break into Mumbai's senior side and to win both Under 23 trophies, in the multi day and T20 formats. Beyond that he wants to play for India, win a World Cup and, now that cricket is part of the Olympics, a gold medal. Away from the field, he would like to learn a few more songs on the piano.
