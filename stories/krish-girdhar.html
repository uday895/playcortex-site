Krish Girdhar

Six Feet Tall and Taking Every Hit

"Just be aggressive and go hard at it."

Krish Girdhar remembers the exact date: 6 December 2016, his first morning practice. Before that, he had done gymnastics, where he could do backflips but couldn't touch his toes. He had watched his friends in the apartment complex play basketball every evening, and his best friend on the school team told him to give it a try.

Earning the Jersey

His school coach was strict. On that team, the saying went, staying on was harder than getting on. Six months in, the coach asked for Krish's jersey size, but he had to win something before his name could go on it. The team went unbeaten in tournaments through 2017. They travelled for tournaments at Woodstock in Mussoorie and Ramjas in Delhi, and the school sent him to camps in Serbia.

His breakout came in a 2018 friendly, when the coach benched the senior players and told the younger ones to go out and play. Krish scored around 28 points, with threes, blocks and moves he had only ever practised alone. "I had no clue I could do all of that." Coaches at the school still bring up that game.

Nearly Walking Away

In eleventh grade, with practice twice a day, seven days a week, he and two teammates wanted to quit. His parents suggested he cut back instead. He took up MUN and other sports, and by the end of the year all three of them were back on court. "You cannot wake up one day and decide to leave the sport."

An Injury That Became a Startup

In his third year of college, a disc bulge led to sciatica that still flares up. For a year and a half he was in and out of hospitals, and some days he could barely get out of bed. He kept a diary of his pain and what helped. That diary turned into a startup idea that won him a government grant from the Ministry of Electronics and IT under Startup India. Looking back, he believes overtraining caused the injury, and he now listens to his body.

Grit Over Size

At six feet, he is undersized for a basketball court, so he takes the hits, dives for loose balls and chases rebounds. He once guarded a 6'8" opponent who had dominated the tournament. The opponent scored two points before fouling out in frustration. "I like to get dirty, dive for every loose ball on the ground."

His advice to his younger self is to be aggressive from day one and not be afraid of mistakes. "Better make mistakes when you're a young guy than when you're 22 about to graduate."
