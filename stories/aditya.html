Aditya

Built by Showing Up

"Just show up. That's it."

Aditya calls himself a hybrid athlete. He is training for a half marathon, has signed up for HYROX doubles, plays badminton and has just finished a high-altitude pass trek. His relationship with sport started with something much harder.

Ten Years Old

When he was ten, a spark during Diwali set his shirt on fire. He was taken first to Malegaon and then to Nashik for surgery. Over the next two to three years he had skin grafts taken from both thighs. For about two years he wore a splint on his arm, at school and everywhere else. His father drove him 12 kilometres to school each day and held an umbrella over him to keep the sun off the wound.

Much later, his family told him that doctors had warned the left side of his body might never fully recover, and that he might not be able to lift his arm. They had kept it from him. "They didn't want it to become a mental block for me. So I never grew up with that limitation in my head."

Sport as Recovery

After the injury, his family pushed him toward sport because his body needed it. He ran track and played badminton under a coach who had played at national level. Years of physiotherapy followed. He was always restless, and the movement suited him.

Two years ago he started going to the gym. For a year and a half nothing seemed to change in how he looked. He kept going anyway. "That taught me that if I don't show up today, the progress I'm working toward simply won't happen."

Finance by Day, Miles by Morning

He has just finished his degree, works in finance and is studying for the CFA. He trains in the morning and studies through the afternoon and evening. Right now that means two or three heart-rate-zone runs a week and the gym every day. He is vegetarian and plans his meals around protein. Sundays are an easy run, because he doesn't believe in a completely idle day.

Outside training, he reads classic literature and philosophy. He started with The Brothers Karamazov.

What Comes Next

First comes the Mumbai half marathon, then a full HYROX event, which he expects will take years to reach competitive level, and one day an ultra-marathon. He plans to join a coached group at Hindu Gymkhana in Pune once his knee and calf have healed.

Asked for five words to describe himself, he chose determined, fearful, strong and peaceful. Fearful, he says, because he's always aware there is more to improve. Strong because on one trek, when everyone else had porters, he carried his own 10 kg rucksack through the snow. And peaceful for the calm he feels after training.

"The real thing is showing up every single day. That's the best I've ever been at anything."
